## Do
Apply the common writing rules to all authored natural-language output. Consult these details for substantial drafting, editing or recurring wording problems; reuse them while they remain in context instead of rereading before every response.

- Use established, searchable terms. Explain unfamiliar terms briefly when the reader needs it; do not invent labels for ordinary concepts. Keep familiar technical terms such as worktree or hook rather than forcing a translation.
- Write naturally in the reader's language. Avoid word-for-word translation and long noun chains. In Korean, replace stacked phrases such as "~에 있어서" and "~에 대해" or passive forms such as "~되어지다" with a clear subject and verb when that makes the sentence easier to read.
- Name the actor, action, relevant condition and observable result. Prefer "The API returns 404 when the order is missing" to "The failure path is handled." Use a concrete example when it helps; do not invent facts or force an example into a simple answer.
- Describe actual behavior directly rather than relying on unnecessary metaphors. Honor an explicit request for analogy, creative writing or a particular style.
- Keep each sentence focused. Split long sentences and remove filler, hype and repeated conclusions. In English, prefer "use" to "utilize" and omit phrases such as "it is worth noting that" when they add nothing. Do not replace a clear sentence with arrows, slogans or an invented compound label.
- Preserve the reasoning a reader needs to decide or understand. A useful recap can remain; shorter text is not automatically clearer. Keep facts, conditions, units, uncertainty and evidence limits intact when editing.
- Preserve quotations, code, identifiers, official names and required output schemas. Do not silently rewrite source material to fit these style rules.

## Check
Can the reader identify who does what, under which conditions, and with what result? Point to unclear terms, forced translations, vague claims or unnecessary metaphors and offer a concrete rewrite. Check that edits retain necessary reasoning and factual qualifications. Judge readability in context rather than enforcing a fixed length or banning every technical term, example or repetition.
